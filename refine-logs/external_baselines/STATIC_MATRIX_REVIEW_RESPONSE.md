# Reviewer-response update: five-domain static comparison

## ECmL F1/F2 and LCo1 W1/W3/P2/P3/P5

We expanded the common-OT static comparison to Economy, Energy, Security, SocialGood and Climate with five seeds (2023–2027), completing 75 evaluation cells across numerical adaptation, static iAdapter and the author GPT4MTS model port. Three pilot cells were reused exactly, and 72 were newly run without changing their training or evaluation rules. The original-CSV audit passed for all 25 dataset/seed groups. This new table is separate from the multivariate online evaluation and does not reproduce the original Table 2.

The results do not support broad iAdapter superiority. Static iAdapter has the lowest mean MSE on 0/5 domains and the lowest mean MAE on 1/5 domains, Security. Relative to numerical adaptation, its mean MSE and MAE are lower only on Security, by approximately 1.16% and 0.23%, respectively. Both mean errors are higher on the other four domains. We retain these negative outcomes and report all seed-level differences; we do not claim statistical significance.

Forecast origins, OT targets, validity masks, chronological splits, and text-availability cutoffs are shared within each domain. The internal alternatives share their seed-specific single-variable backbone and use a fixed tail insertion point. GPT4MTS retains the author architecture, without an insertion-position search or averaging. Error bars are sample standard deviations over seeds only. They no longer mix insertion-position variation with run variation.

The comparison still has clearly identified design differences: iAdapter consumes the currently selected pooled text, whereas GPT4MTS consumes a historical text sequence with CLS/512 encoding. Internal adapters are warmed on the training stream before freezing; GPT4MTS uses batch supervised training and validation selection. We therefore describe this as a controlled common-task system comparison, not an equal-text-budget intervention or an isolated MoE effect. All methods remain static during development scoring, with internal feedback reset to zero and held fixed. These results cannot establish the benefit or failure of online adaptation.

The data split is 60% training, 60%–70% checkpoint selection and 70%–80% development evaluation, with H=6, lookback 48 for Energy and 36 otherwise. These are optimization repeats on previously examined data, not independent-data confirmation. Seed labels align the recorded comparisons, but the different architectures do not share initial parameters. Overlapping targets are not treated as independent repetitions. Text availability remains an end_date proxy rather than verified publication time.

VoT's generated-prior provenance is still unresolved, so it is absent from this table. We do not replace it with the supplied proxy under the original method name. The unmatched original superiority statement should be withdrawn; a native external online comparison requires a separately declared online extension and common delayed-update executor. That extension has not yet been evaluated in this static table.
